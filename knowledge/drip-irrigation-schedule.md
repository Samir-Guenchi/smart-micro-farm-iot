# Drip irrigation schedules for vegetables

Drip puts water at the roots and uses about 30–60% less than sprinklers. Water deeply and less often, so roots grow down.

**Starting schedule (adjust to soil and weather):**

| Crop | Weekly water in warm weather | Critical stage |
|---|---|---|
| Tomato | 20–30 mm (20–30 L per m²) | Flowering and fruit swelling; keep even to avoid blossom end rot |
| Lettuce | 15–25 mm, little and often | Whole life; shallow roots dry fast |
| Potato | 20–30 mm | From flowering, when tubers form |
| Corn | 25–35 mm | Tasselling and silking |
| Broad bean | 10–20 mm | Flowering and pod set |

1 mm of water over 1 m² is 1 litre.

**Rules of thumb:**
- Water in the early morning; less evaporation and leaves stay dry.
- Sandy soil: shorter, more frequent runs. Clay: longer, less frequent.
- Mulch cuts water needs by a quarter or more.
- In heat waves above 35 °C (North Africa), needs can double.
- Check by digging: the soil should be moist 15–20 cm down a few hours after watering.
- Best of all, water by soil moisture, not the clock; see the soil moisture note.
