# Varroa mite: monitoring and treatment

Varroa mites are the main cause of colony loss. Every colony has them; the aim is to keep numbers low.

**Monitoring:** count mites at least in spring and late summer.
- Sugar roll: shake about 300 bees (half a cup) from a brood frame into a jar with powdered sugar, roll, and count the mites that fall through a mesh lid. More than about 3 mites per 100 bees in late summer means treat now.
- Or a sticky board under a mesh floor for 3 days.

**Treatments used in organic beekeeping** (always follow the product label and national rules):
- Formic acid after the honey harvest in late summer, when temperatures are in the product's range.
- Oxalic acid in winter when the colony has no brood, since mites in capped brood are not reached.
- Drone brood removal in spring: mites prefer drone cells; cut out capped drone comb.

**Never** treat with honey supers on, and rotate methods to avoid resistance.
