# Pruning the perimeter fruit trees

| Tree | When | How |
|---|---|---|
| Apricot, plum | Summer, after harvest | Remove dead, crossing and inward branches; avoid winter cuts, which let in silver leaf and canker. |
| Peach | At flowering (late winter) | Fruit grows on last year's shoots; each year cut back about a third of the old fruited shoots to new ones. Keep an open goblet shape. |
| Pomegranate | Late winter, while dormant | Remove suckers from the base; keep 3–5 trunks. |
| Citrus | After harvest, spring | Light pruning only: remove dead wood, water shoots and branches touching the ground. |
| Mulberry | Mid winter, fully dormant | Minimal pruning; it bleeds sap if cut in spring. |

**Always:** use clean, sharp tools, disinfect between trees if disease is present, and cut just above an outward-facing bud.

**Windbreak role:** the perimeter trees also shelter the vegetable beds; keep the outer side dense and prune mainly for light on the inner side.
