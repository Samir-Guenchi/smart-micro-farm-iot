# Composting poultry manure

Fresh hen and quail manure is very rich in nitrogen and can burn plants and carry pathogens. Compost it before it goes on vegetable beds.

**How:**
- Mix one part manure (with its bedding) with two to three parts brown, carbon-rich material: straw, dry leaves, wood shavings, shredded cardboard.
- Build a pile at least 1 m × 1 m × 1 m so it heats up. Keep it as damp as a wrung-out sponge.
- It should reach 55–65 °C within a few days. Turn it every week or two for the first month, when the centre cools.
- After 2–3 months of hot composting and a further 2–3 months of curing, it is dark and smells of earth.

**Safety:** for crops eaten raw (lettuce), use only fully composted manure, and apply it at least 4 months before harvest. Wear gloves and wash hands after handling.

**Using it:** spread 2–3 cm on beds in autumn or before planting. Hungry crops (tomato, corn, potato) benefit most; beans need little.
