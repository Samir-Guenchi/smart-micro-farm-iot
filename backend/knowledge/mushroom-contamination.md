# Mushroom growing: contamination and green mould

**What it looks like:** green, black, orange or pink patches on the substrate instead of white mycelium; sour or rotten smell.

**Why it happens:** competing moulds (often *Trichoderma*, green) get in before the mushroom mycelium has covered the substrate.

**What to do:**
- Pasteurise straw in hot water at 65–80 °C for an hour, or with a lime soak, then drain and cool before adding spawn.
- Work clean: wash hands, wipe surfaces with alcohol, and keep the incubation room tidy.
- Use plenty of spawn (about 5–10% of the wet substrate weight) so the mycelium wins the race.
- Incubate in the dark at 20–24 °C for oyster mushrooms until fully white, then move to fruiting conditions (cooler, 85–95% humidity, fresh air, some light).
- Remove contaminated bags at once, away from the growing room.
- In hot climates, above 25–28 °C contamination rises sharply: grow in the cool season or in a cool cellar.
