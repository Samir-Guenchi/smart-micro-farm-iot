# Colorado potato beetle

**What it looks like:** yellow beetles with black stripes, orange egg clusters under the leaves, and fat red-orange larvae that strip the foliage.

**What to do:**
- From when shoots appear, check plants twice a week. Hand-pick adults into soapy water and crush egg clusters.
- The first generation in spring is the one to stop; it is small and easy to catch.
- Cover the bed with fine mesh right after planting if the beetle was bad last year.
- Rotate potatoes as far as possible from last year's bed; adults walk to new plants.
- Poultry will eat some larvae, but hens also scratch up the bed; let them in only after harvest.
- Bacterial sprays based on *Bacillus thuringiensis tenebrionis* target young larvae; follow the label.
