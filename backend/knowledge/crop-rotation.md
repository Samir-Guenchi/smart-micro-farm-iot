# Crop rotation for the vegetable beds

Moving each crop family to a new bed every year stops soil diseases and pests building up and balances nutrients.

**A simple four-bed rotation:**

| Year | Bed A | Bed B | Bed C | Bed D |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Potatoes and tomatoes (nightshades) | Beans and peas | Cabbages, roots | Corn, squash, lettuce |
| 2 | Corn, squash, lettuce | Nightshades | Beans | Cabbages, roots |

…and so on, moving one bed each year.

**Why this order:** nightshades are hungry and get the most compost; beans follow and add nitrogen; leafy and root crops use what is left; corn and squash take fresh compost again.

**Rules:**
- Wait at least 3 years before tomatoes or potatoes return to a bed.
- Keep a simple record of what grew where each year; the progress log is a good place.
- Sow a green manure (phacelia, mustard, vetch) on beds that would otherwise be bare over winter.
