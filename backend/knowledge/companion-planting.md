# Companion planting on this farm

Some plants grow better together, mostly because they share space well, attract helpful insects or confuse pests.

**Pairs that work:**
- **Tomato + basil, marigold (Tagetes) or calendula:** flowers bring hoverflies and ladybirds; marigolds may reduce some soil nematodes.
- **Lettuce between tomatoes or corn:** grows in their shade in summer and is harvested before they fill the space.
- **Broad beans before corn or tomatoes:** beans fix nitrogen; cut them at ground level and leave the roots.
- **Potato + broad beans or tagetes along the edge.**
- **Phacelia, dill, alyssum, borage in or around beds:** feed bees and the predators of aphids.
- **Corn + beans + squash:** see the three sisters note.

**Pairs to avoid:**
- Potato near tomato: share blight and pests.
- Corn and tomato in the same row: both attract the corn earworm.
- Fennel next to most vegetables; it slows their growth.

Rotation matters more than companions: never grow the same family in the same bed two years running.
