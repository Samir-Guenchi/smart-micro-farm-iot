# Late blight on tomatoes and potatoes

**What it looks like:** brown-black patches on leaves that spread fast in warm, wet weather, often with white fuzz underneath. Stems turn black, and tomato fruit gets firm brown patches. A whole bed can collapse in a week.

**Why it happens:** a water mould (*Phytophthora infestans*). It spreads on wind and rain splash when nights stay above 10 °C and leaves stay wet for 10 hours or more.

**What to do:**
- Water at the base with drip, never overhead, and water in the morning so leaves dry.
- Space plants and remove lower leaves on tomatoes for airflow.
- Grow tomatoes under a roof (polytunnel or rain cover) in wet climates like Latvia.
- At the first spots, remove and bag affected leaves; do not compost them.
- If potato foliage is badly hit, cut the haulm off and remove it, then wait two weeks before lifting so spores on the soil do not reach the tubers.
- Choose resistant varieties where you can.
- Copper sprays slow it in organic growing; follow the label and local rules.

**Prevention next year:** rotate, do not plant tomatoes next to potatoes, and remove volunteer potatoes that sprout from last year's tubers.
