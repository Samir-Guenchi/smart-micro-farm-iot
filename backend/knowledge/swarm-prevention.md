# Preventing and catching swarms

A swarm takes half the bees and the old queen, and the honey crop with them.

**Warning signs:** queen cells along the bottom edges of frames, a crowded brood box, bees hanging outside on warm evenings. On the hive scale, a sudden drop of 1–3 kg in the middle of a sunny day is likely a swarm leaving.

**Prevention:**
- Inspect every 7–9 days in swarming season.
- Give space early: add a super before the bees need it.
- Make a split: move the old queen with a few frames of brood and stores to a new hive; the old hive raises a new queen.
- Choose calm, low-swarming stock when requeening.

**Catching:** a swarm on a branch is usually calm. Shake it into a box with a few frames, leave the box nearby until evening so the rest join, then move it to a new hive.
